What the Neck is, and what we could verify

Marblehead Neck is a rocky peninsula described as a near island, joined to the mainland by a long causeway. Marblehead Light and Chandler Hovey Park sit at its northern tip, and the Marblehead Neck Association is one of the neighborhood groups the town's community page names.

The state's 1985 survey says the Neck became a summer resort from 1867, with roads laid out between 1872 and 1884, and that its Atlantic side later became a corridor of large coastal estates. Today the Neck is a residential area, but we found no source for how many homes are seasonal.

Lot size, shoreline and what the town plan says

The town's master plan places the Neck and its northern tip in zoning districts that require 35,000 square foot minimum lots for single family homes. Larger lots can mean longer runs between a house and the street, which is worth mentioning on the call.

The same plan rates the Neck and shoreline lowest for new housing partly because of flood and sea level rise risk. That is a planning finding about the area, not a statement about any one basement, so ask about conditions at your own address.

Coastal conditions in general

Any shoreline house deals with salt air on outdoor fixtures, wind in winter and, in some places, groundwater after heavy rain. These are general plumbing points. Exposed pipes and hose bibs are the usual places for a winter freeze, and a house left empty is the likeliest to find a leak late.

If the house is closed for part of the year, ask a plumber about shutting off and draining the water before you leave, and about who checks the property. A sump pump that fails during a storm is worth testing before the season.

Planning a visit across the causeway

The Neck is reached by the causeway, so route and timing matter. Give the dispatcher the exact address, any gate or access instructions and what the problem is. A plumber who knows the parts needed brings them in one trip.
