Emergency: what to do first when a drain backs up

Sewage or gray water coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet is a health and property problem. Treat standing water as contaminated, keep children and pets out and avoid touching it without gloves.

Our emergency plumbers handle after-hours backups the same way they handle daytime calls, with a same-day visit when available. The steps below limit the damage until they arrive.

Why Marblehead drains need a careful hand

Marblehead is a small, densely built peninsula, and the town's own history says the post-war building boom ended around 1970. That leaves a large stock of homes with drain pipe old enough to be cast iron, clay or early plastic. Old pipe scales on the inside, which narrows the bore and gives grease something to hold on to.

That is why a plumber working in Old Town or Clifton should ask the age of the house before choosing a tool. If the stack turns out to be corroded cast iron, it does not tolerate a heavy cutting head, so the crew starts gentler and escalates only if the line needs it.

Clearing a clog follows ordinary trade practice, but replacing a section of drain pipe is plumbing work held to the standards of the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00. If a job goes beyond clearing, ask the Marblehead Building Department whether a permit applies.

Kitchen sink clogs and why they come back

Kitchen drains clog because grease cools inside the pipe and traps food scraps against it. A sink that drains slowly for a week and then stops has usually built a layer inside the first ten feet of pipe, past the trap. Hot water alone pushes the grease further down where it sets again.

A hand snake clears the plug, but the coating on the pipe wall remains and the clog returns. For a kitchen line that has backed up more than once, we ask the plumber to run a cutting head on the cable, which scrapes the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole through the plug.

If the kitchen sink shares a line with a dishwasher and disposal, the clog can sit past the point where all three join. That is one reason we check the dishwasher drain hose and the disposal before assuming the sink line is the only problem.

Snaking, cabling and what each one is for

Homeowners say snaking for everything, but the tools differ. A hand auger reaches about 25 feet and fits a sink or tub trap. A drum machine pushes a quarter-inch to three-eighths-inch cable through a three-inch branch. A larger sectional machine drives a half-inch cable with cutting heads into a four-inch main line.

Roots, scale and hardened grease need a cutting head, not a bare cable. A bare cable punches a hole, water drains for a few days and then the line closes again. That pattern is the signal that the clog was never fully removed.

When a snake stops and will not advance, the plumber should say what the machine is hitting. A bellied pipe, an offset joint or a collapsed section feels different from a root mass, and a camera settles the question.

A drain line that leaks does not always show as a clog. A damp spot below a tub drain, a stained ceiling under a bathroom or a musty cabinet can all trace to a cracked drain line, and we check for that when the stain and the clog show up together.
