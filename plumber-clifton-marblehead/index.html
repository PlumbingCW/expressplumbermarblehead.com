What Clifton is, and what we could verify

Clifton lies in the southwest of Marblehead along the border with Swampscott. The Clifton Improvement Association is one of the neighborhood groups the town's community page lists, so the name is in current local use.

A 2020 survey for the Marblehead Historical Commission covers the Clifton Heights area between Humphrey Street and the ocean, with Atlantic Avenue as its main route. It records 167 buildings across the Greystone Beach and Greystone Heights subdivisions, and it describes the area as mostly residential.

Three house eras, three starting questions

The survey describes resort era houses from the late 1800s, smaller houses from the years after the First World War, and two story colonials, ranches and split levels from after the Second World War. Greystone Beach was filed in 1926, Greystone Heights in 1928 and Greystone Beach Section II in 1959.

For a plumber, the useful question is which era a house belongs to, because a house of each era was built to different practice and may have been changed since. We do not assume what is inside a wall from the street. We look, then explain what we see.

Where the 1870s railroad village fits

The state's 1985 survey places Clifton Village, built up between 1870 and 1880 near the Clifton depot, south of the railroad and west of Rockaway Avenue. Clifton Heights and Clifton Village are separate parts of the area, and boundaries are not exact, so tell us your street on the call.

General guidance before a storm or a cold snap

These points apply to any house, not only those in Clifton. Find the main water shutoff and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is. Test a sump pump by lifting the float before heavy rain, and check that the discharge line is clear.

In winter, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze and keep the heat on in a house you leave. If a pipe does burst, shut off the water first and then call.
