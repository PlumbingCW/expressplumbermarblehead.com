The plumbing problems Swampscott homeowners call about

Water heaters that quit

A tank that goes cold or starts leaking is the most common emergency call from any coastal town. Swampscott homes range from older shingle houses to later construction, and tanks in damp basements near the water tend to corrode early. See emergency water heater replacement and repair.

Drains that back up

Repeat kitchen and main-line clogs show up in older houses with cast iron or clay drains, and in houses with trees close to the sewer lateral. Start with drain cleaning. If it keeps returning, a camera inspection finds the reason.

Leaks you cannot see

A meter that keeps turning, a ceiling stain or a bill that jumped usually means a hidden leak. Leak detection traces it with pressure tests and thermal imaging before anyone opens a wall.

Swampscott's water system and what it means for your pipes

Swampscott buys all of its water from the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority. The town's Department of Public Works runs the local distribution system, which the town's own 2021 water letter describes as roughly 57 miles of mains. According to that letter, about 70 percent of the pipe is more than fifty years old, and about 60 percent is unlined cast iron.

The same letter explains that unlined cast iron can build up rust deposits over time, and the town has been cleaning and lining mains such as the 10-inch main on Atlantic Avenue. What that means inside a house is simple. Water arriving through an older main can carry more sediment, and the first places it shows up are aerators, water heater tanks and shower valves.

Humphrey Street, Atlantic Avenue and coastal conditions

Humphrey Street and Atlantic Avenue run along the Swampscott shore, and houses close to the water deal with salt air, damp basements and wind-driven cold in winter. Exposed hose bibs and pipes in exterior walls are the usual places for a freeze, so a cold-snap burst is a real risk. See burst pipe repair for what the first hour looks like.

Basements near the shore take in groundwater after heavy rain. A sump pump that fails in a storm is an emergency, and sump pump repair covers the checks and the backup options.

What to do while the plumber is on the way to Swampscott

The drive from Marblehead is short, about 3 miles by the coast road as an estimate, but a few minutes of preparation limits the damage. These steps cover the most common emergencies.

  • Water: Close the main shutoff, usually near the meter in the basement.
  • Power: Switch off breakers serving any wet room.
  • Drains: Stop running sinks, showers and laundry if a drain is backing up.
  • Gas: If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Sewer backups and older laterals

Several drains slowing at once, a gurgling tub or a floor drain that smells point to the main line. Sewer line cleaning clears the blockage, and hydro jetting scours the line when grease and scale keep coming back.

Ask the Swampscott Department of Public Works where the town's sewer begins and your responsibility ends for your address. We do not guess at that boundary, and the town can confirm it before a dig.

Permits for plumbing work in Swampscott

Plumbing work in Massachusetts follows the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and many jobs need a permit pulled by a licensed plumber. Ask the Swampscott building office what applies to your project. We tell you at the visit whether a permit is likely, so you are not surprised.
