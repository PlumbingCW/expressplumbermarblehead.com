Emergency leak detection in Marblehead, MA

Water you cannot see is leaking, and our line is answered 24/7. Shut off the main water valve if a ceiling is dripping or a wall is wet, and call. Same-day visits are offered when a slot is available.

Leak detection means finding the exact location of a hidden leak using pressure tests, listening equipment, thermal imaging and tracer methods, so the repair opens as little of the house as possible. Most callers describe a symptom, not a leak. The meter will not stop turning, the water bill went up, a ceiling stained, or a floor feels warm.

Emergency: active leak and what to do first

Water that is actively dripping through a ceiling or spraying from a wall is an emergency, and the main shutoff is the first move. After that, switch off power to the wet area from the breaker panel, because water and wiring do not mix.

The emergency line answers 24/7, and same-day visits are offered when available. The steps below limit damage while you wait.

  • Main valve offTurn the main water shutoff clockwise.
  • Power offSwitch off breakers serving the wet area.
  • Catch and moveCatch drips and move furniture and electronics.
  • Call the lineTell the dispatcher what is wet and where.

Leaks in older, coastal homes

Marblehead's housing stock leans old, and the town's own history notes that building slowed to a stop around 1970. A home from that era or earlier may still carry galvanized supply lines or early copper with old solder. Both can leak at joints after decades of pressure and temperature cycling.

In its published water report, the town says it has no known lead service lines, but a few homes have galvanized iron services that may include a short lead gooseneck connection. The water department can confirm your service material and the year it was installed.

Leak testing follows accepted trade practice, such as isolating a section and holding a pressure reading. Any repair that follows is held to the standards of the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, so ask for the written finding of where the leak was.

Meter still running with everything off

Turn off every tap, the ice maker and the washing machine, then look at the water meter. If the leak indicator or low-flow dial still moves, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. That test separates a plumbing leak from a toilet or irrigation fault.

A toilet that is silently refilling is the most common culprit, and it is easy to check with a few drops of dye in the tank. If the bowl shows dye without flushing, the flapper is leaking and the fix is cheap.

If every fixture is ruled out, the leak is in a buried or concealed line. The plumber then isolates sections with shutoffs and pressure tests each one.

High water bill with no obvious cause

A bill that jumps with no change in habits is worth a leak check, but billing periods vary, so compare the same months across years first. Ask the water department about the meter reading history, and read your own meter at the same time each day to see a pattern.

Marblehead's water comes from the MWRA, billed through the town. The town's water department can tell you about meter issues, and a plumber can tell you about leaks on your side of the meter.

Wet ceilings, walls and warm floors

A stain on a ceiling under a bathroom can be a supply line, a drain or the shower pan. A warm spot on a slab or under hardwood can be a hot water line. A musty smell in a closet can be a slow leak behind a wall.

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the damp area, then confirm with a pressure test. The access opening is then as small as the repair allows.

Slab, buried and under-house leaks

A leak under a concrete slab or in a buried supply line needs more than a visual check. Acoustic listening equipment hears the sound of water escaping, and a tracer gas test can confirm the spot. Crawlspace leaks are easier to see but harder to reach.

We also look for yard and meter-box leaks, irrigation lines and sprinkler valves, and we tell you when a leak is on the town's side of the meter, which is not a plumber's repair.

What to document for insurance

  • Photos of the stain or damp area, with the date.
  • Meter readings before and after the shutoff test.
  • The plumber's written finding of the source and the date it was found.
  • Receipts for repairs and any emergency mitigation.
