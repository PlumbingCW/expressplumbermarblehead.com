Tankless water heater service in Marblehead, MA

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it has no storage tank and a much smaller footprint. In Marblehead the common calls are for new installs, error codes and flushing units that have lost output to scale. We handle all three, and we tell you honestly when a unit is not the right fit for the house.

Tankless units last about 20 years when maintained, compared with 8 to 12 for a standard tank. That longevity depends on flushing, because scale narrows the heat exchanger and cuts flow over time.

Tankless in compact Marblehead homes

Small basements, tight closets and narrow stairs are common in Marblehead, and that is where a wall-mounted tankless unit earns its keep. The plumber checks the wall location, the vent path to the exterior and the clearance to combustibles before recommending one.

In a house that was built before the town was fully built out in the 1970s, venting through an old chimney or an exterior wall needs care. We check what is already there before we price the job.

Flushing and descaling: the service most units miss

Calcium and magnesium in water coat the heat exchanger of a tankless heater, and the unit responds by overheating, throwing an error code or delivering lukewarm water. A flush pumps a descaling solution through the exchanger, then rinses it, and typically takes an hour or so.

Many manufacturers recommend flushing once a year, and the interval can be shorter in hard water. Marblehead's water is supplied by the MWRA, and we do not state a hardness figure here, so ask the town or read the annual water report for your own number.

Common error codes and what they suggest

Error codes differ by brand, so we read the manual and the unit's history before opening anything. Ignition failures often trace to a gas supply or a dirty flame sensor. Flow faults often trace to scale, a clogged inlet filter or a failed flow sensor. Vent faults point to a blocked or damaged vent run.

Do not clear a persistent code repeatedly and hope. If a code returns after a reset, the cause is still present, and operating the unit that way can damage the exchanger.

Sizing and gas line checks for a new install

A gas tankless unit often needs a larger gas line than the tank it replaces, and a vent run rated for the unit. We check the gas meter capacity and the line length so the unit gets enough fuel at full firing rate.

Electric tankless units draw a very large current, and many older panels cannot supply it. We flag that early so you can get an electrician's quote before committing to the unit.

Cold water sandwich and other quirks

Tankless users sometimes feel a burst of cold water between two uses, called the cold water sandwich. It happens because the unit shuts off between draws and the pipe holds cooled water. A recirculation line or a small buffer tank can reduce it, and we discuss those options if it bothers you.
