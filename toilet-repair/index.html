Toilet repair in Marblehead, MA

A toilet that is overflowing or leaking onto the floor is an emergency, and our line is answered 24/7. Turn the valve behind the bowl clockwise, lift the tank lid and close the flapper, and call for a same-day visit when a slot is open.

Toilet repair covers the common faults: a tank that refills constantly, a bowl that clogs, a base that leaks or a seat that rocks. Most are fixable in a single visit, and small leaks are worth fixing early, because a toilet leaking at the base rots the subfloor slowly.

If a toilet is overflowing, shut the valve behind the bowl by turning it clockwise and call. If it keeps clogging, the cause may be in the drain line, and we say so instead of selling a new toilet.

Emergency: an overflowing or leaking toilet

Overflow water from a toilet bowl is contaminated, so keep children and pets away and wear gloves when you handle it. Stopping the supply is the first move.

Toilets in Marblehead's older bathrooms

Older Marblehead homes often have small bathrooms with fixtures that have been replaced several times, and floors that have moved with the house. A toilet that rocks may be on an uneven or soft floor, not just loose bolts.

Water supply comes from the MWRA, and the practical effect for a toilet is the usual one. Flappers and fill valves wear out, and a plumber should replace them as a pair on an old tank so you are not back for the second one.

Toilets and their connections are installed according to the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and the manufacturer's guidelines. A repair inside the fixture is one thing and moving a toilet or its drain is another, so ask the Building Department whether that needs a permit.

A toilet that keeps running

The flapper is the rubber seal at the bottom of the tank, and it hardens or warps with age. Water seeps past it and the tank refills, hour after hour. A few drops of food coloring in the tank, left for twenty minutes, will show color in the bowl if the flapper is leaking.

Fill valves also wear. A float that is set too high sends water down the overflow tube. Both are cheap parts, and the fix is often a quick one.

Clogs and slow flushes

A flush that starts strong and ends weak can mean a partial clog, a partly blocked rim jet or a vent problem on the drain line. A plunger with a flange fits the toilet outlet better than a sink plunger.

A toilet that clogs often, even after plunging, may have a blockage further along. If other fixtures slow too, see drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning.

When to replace instead of repair

A cracked bowl, a chronic clogger or a toilet that uses far more water than modern ones is a candidate for replacement. Newer toilets are designed to flush well on a modest volume of water. We help you choose a height and bowl shape that suit the household, and we remove the old one.

Mold, rot and the subfloor

If a leak went unnoticed, the subfloor may be soft. Press on the floor beside the toilet. A spongy feel means rot, and the toilet should come up so the floor can be checked and repaired before the new wax ring goes in.
