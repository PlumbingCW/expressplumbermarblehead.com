Emergency Plumber Service Built Around a Rocky Peninsula

When a Marblehead plumbing problem cannot wait

A burst supply line, a toilet overflowing into a finished room, or a water heater leaking onto a basement floor does not wait for business hours. Our emergency plumber line is open 24/7, and the person who answers can book a plumber for the same day when a slot is available.

While you wait for the visit, shut off the main water valve, switch off the power to any wet room from the breaker panel, and move rugs and electronics out of the path of the water. Stop running drains if a sewer backup is the problem. Those three steps often limit the damage more than the repair itself.

Call the 24/7 line at (339) 216-9771 for any of the 21 services listed below, from a failed tank to a cracked drain stack. If the problem is a gas smell, leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.

Plumbing across Old Town, the Neck and Clifton

Marblehead sits on a small, rocky peninsula, and the town is densely built. Old Town is the original settlement with narrow streets, and much of it falls inside the Marblehead Historic District, where houses date back centuries. In an older home like that, a plumber may plan around crawlspaces, boxed-in pipe runs and stone foundations before touching a fitting.

Marblehead Neck is joined to the mainland by a causeway, so a job there means planning the route and the parts before the van leaves. Clifton, along the Swampscott line, mixes resort era houses with later subdivisions. Homes in town range from colonial era houses to postwar construction, so the first question on any call is the age of the house, not an assumption about what is behind the wall.

That matters for what you pay and how long a repair lasts. A leak on a fifty-year-old galvanized line is rarely a one-fitting fix, so the plumber shows you the pipe condition and quote the repair and the replacement side by side.

What homeowners call about most

The calls that fill the plumbing trade are consistent. A water meter that keeps turning with every tap closed, a water bill that jumps with no explanation, a wet ceiling under a bathroom, a water heater leaking at the base, and a drain that backs up again two weeks after it was snaked. Each one has its own page on this site with the symptoms, the likely causes and what the fix involves.

Start with leak detection if the meter is moving, drain cleaning for repeat clogs, and water heater repair or water heater replacement when the hot water fails. A tank past ten years old is usually a replacement conversation, not a repair one.

Check any plumber's licence before work starts

According to the state, plumbing work in Massachusetts is licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, which issues journeyman and master plumber licences. The state publishes a Check a license tool on mass.gov, and you can use it to confirm any plumber before they open a wall.

Marblehead's Building Department also states that plumbing permits are issued to licensed plumbers, and that starting work without a required permit triggers a fee of three times the normal amount. Ask whoever you hire who is pulling the permit. A plumber who hesitates on that question is telling you something.
