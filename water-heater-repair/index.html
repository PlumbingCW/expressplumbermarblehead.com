Emergency water heater repair in Marblehead, MA

No hot water in the morning is a household emergency, and our line is answered 24/7. Check the breaker or the gas supply and note any error light, then call. A same-day repair visit is available when a slot opens.

Water heater repair means finding which part failed, replacing it and testing the unit. The usual culprits are a heating element, thermostat, gas control valve, thermocouple, igniter, relief valve or anode rod. A good diagnosis prevents replacing parts that were not the cause.

Emergency: no hot water or a leaking heater

A heater leaking from a fitting can flood a basement just as a failed tank can. Cutting the supply and the power or gas takes under a minute and prevents most of the damage.

Water heaters in a coastal, older housing stock

Marblehead is a coastal peninsula with many older homes, and basements and utility closets in those houses tend to be cold in winter and humid in summer. Cold incoming water makes a heater work harder in January, and humidity corrodes exposed fittings and the base of the tank.

The town's water comes entirely from the MWRA, and the plumber's practical advice is to check the anode rod and flush the tank on a schedule rather than wait for rumbling. We note the age and condition of each unit we repair so you know where it stands.

Water heater work follows the manufacturer's guidelines and the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and gas appliances are also held to the state's fuel gas standards. Ask which licence covers the gas side of the work.

No hot water: a short diagnostic path

Electric tanks usually fail at the upper or lower element or at a thermostat. If the water is lukewarm, a failed lower element is the common cause. If there is no hot water at all, the upper element, the thermostat or a tripped high-limit reset is more likely.

Gas tanks lose heat when the pilot goes out, the thermocouple fails or the gas control valve stops opening. A pilot that will not stay lit even with a new thermocouple points to a failing valve or a draft problem in the flue.

Leaks: fitting or tank

Water on the floor near the tank is not always the tank. A weeping inlet or outlet fitting, a relief valve discharge or a drain valve can all drip, and those are repairable. Condensation on a cold basement day can mimic a leak too.

If the shell itself is wet, or water seeps from the bottom seam, the tank has failed. The plumber checks the fittings first and then tells you whether water heater replacement is the honest answer.

When to call about these signs

Rumbling or popping

Sediment on the tank bottom boils and bangs. A flush often helps if the tank is young.

Rotten egg smell

A reaction with the anode rod can cause it, and the plumber tests the rod first.

Breaker keeps tripping

A shorted element or a loose wire is the usual cause. Do not keep resetting it.

Pilot keeps going out

A weak thermocouple, a draft or a dirty burner assembly need a plumber.

Hybrid, electric and gas units

Hybrid heat pump heaters have their own failure modes, including condensate drains, fans, compressor faults and error codes. We look at what the unit itself reports before opening anything, and we tell you when the manufacturer's service channel is the better route.

For gas tanks, safety comes first. A gas smell, a sooty flue or a yellow burner flame is a reason to shut the unit off and call, and we do not return power to a unit that is venting badly.

After-hours repair

The most common after-hours repair calls are no hot water and a leaking relief valve. We offer same-day service when available, and the dispatcher tells you what is safe to do in the meantime.
