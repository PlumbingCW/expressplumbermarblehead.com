Plumbing installation in Marblehead, MA

Plumbing installation covers the planned jobs: putting in a new sink, toilet, shower or tub, adding a laundry hookup, running a line to a new bathroom or moving a fixture during a remodel. The work includes the supply lines, the drain and vent, the shutoff valves and the permit where one is needed.

A good installation starts with a plan. We look at where the water supply, drain and vent can reach, whether the existing pipes have enough capacity and whether the floor or wall can take the new route.

Remodeling in Marblehead homes

Marblehead remodels often work within old walls and tight footprints. Moving a bathroom in a house with a granite foundation or narrow joist bays takes planning, and the drain route may decide the layout before the design does.

In historic parts of town, exterior changes may need local approval, so check with the Building Department before work that touches the outside of the house.

Rough-in and finish

Rough-in is the stage when the supply and drain lines are placed in the open walls and floor, before drywall and tile. The finish stage connects the fixtures after the surfaces are done. Mistakes at rough-in are expensive to fix later, so we measure to the fixture specification and confirm heights before closing walls.

If you are working with a general contractor, we coordinate timing so the plumbing is inspected before the walls are covered.

Moving or adding a fixture

Moving a toilet or a shower means moving the drain, and a drain needs slope and a vent. Where the floor joists run decides how far a drain can go without cutting into structure. Adding a bathroom in a basement may need a sewage ejector pump if the drain line is above the new fixtures.

We explain what is possible before you commit to a layout.

Fixture choices that save headaches

Standard sizes

Fixtures with common rough-in dimensions have easier repairs later.

Accessible shutoffs

Every fixture should have its own stop valve.

Quality supply lines

Braided stainless lines resist bursting.

Matching the finish

Order trim and valves from one line to avoid compatibility issues.

Permits and inspection

Massachusetts plumbing work follows the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00. Marblehead's Building Department issues plumbing permits to licensed plumbers, and starting without a required permit triggers a fee of three times the normal amount. We ask whether a permit applies before work starts.

Appliances and outdoor lines

Dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers and outdoor spigots all connect to the plumbing. We install the supply and drain connections, add shutoff valves and use backflow protection where required. For outdoor lines, we install frost-proof spigots that drain themselves in cold weather.
