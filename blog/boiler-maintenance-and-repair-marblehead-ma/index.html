Boiler Maintenance and Repair in Marblehead, MA Homes

A boiler that gets a yearly check fails less and costs less to run. Here is what the check includes and which faults you can spot yourself.

Why yearly boiler service matters on the North Shore

A boiler heats water or makes steam and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboard. It runs hard from late fall through early spring, and a failure in January is an emergency. A yearly service before the heating season catches worn parts before they stop the system.

Many Marblehead houses are old and have boilers that have been patched and extended over decades. That is not a reason to replace them, but it is a reason to keep them serviced.

What a yearly service includes

  • Check the pressure, the relief valve and the expansion tank.
  • Inspect the burner or igniter and clean it as needed.
  • Test the circulator pump and zone valves.
  • Check the flue and venting and test combustion.
  • Look for leaks at fittings and the boiler body.

Checks you can do yourself

Read the pressure gauge. Most residential hot water boilers sit in the low teens when cold, though your manual gives the exact range. Pressure that keeps falling means a leak, and pressure that climbs and discharges the relief valve points to the expansion tank.

Feel each radiator after the system has run for a while. A radiator that is cold at the top has trapped air and needs bleeding. A radiator cold at the bottom may be clogged with sludge.

Local note

Local note: licences and permits for heating work in Marblehead

In Massachusetts, plumbing and gas fitting are licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, and the state's Check a license tool on mass.gov lets you verify anyone before work starts. Marblehead's Building Department lists permits for plumbing and gas work and says they are issued to licensed professionals.

Ask your service company who is licensed for the work and who pulls any required permit. A boiler replacement is a bigger job than a service call, and the Building Department can say what it needs.

Pressure ranges and service intervals follow the manufacturer's guidelines in your boiler's manual, so keep the manual with the unit and read it before each season.

Bleeding a radiator

With the boiler off and cool, open the bleed valve at the top of the radiator with a radiator key. Hold a cloth under it and let the air hiss out until water appears, then close it. Check the boiler pressure afterward and top up if the manual allows.

If you are not sure, ask a plumber to show you once. It takes a minute.

Warning signs that need a plumber

Banging or kettling

Scale or low flow overheats water in the exchanger.

Repeated lockouts

An ignition, fuel or venting fault.

Yellow flame or soot

Incomplete combustion is a safety issue.

Water at the base

A leaking fitting or a failing boiler body.

Carbon monoxide and safety

A working carbon monoxide detector near the boiler room and on each floor is essential. If the detector sounds or you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. See boiler repair for what a no-heat call involves.

Steam versus hot water systems

A steam boiler boils water into steam that rises through pipes to radiators and condenses back to water. It needs a sight glass check, a low-water cutoff test and working radiator vents. A hot water boiler pumps heated water through a closed loop and relies on pressure, circulators and an expansion tank.

Know which you have, because the maintenance differs. A plumber can tell you in a moment, and your service visit should be matched to the type.

Costs of skipping service

Skipped service shows up as higher fuel bills, more breakdowns and, in the worst case, a safety fault. A dirty burner burns less efficiently and can produce soot. A sticking relief valve fails when pressure spikes.

A yearly visit costs far less than an emergency call in the coldest week of the year.

Choosing a service company

Ask whether the technician is licensed for the work, whether the visit includes a combustion test and what is checked on the written report. A good report lists the readings and any parts that are near the end of life.

Ask what the company does after hours. A heat failure on a holiday weekend is when you will need a real answer.
