Garbage disposal repair in Marblehead, MA

A disposal leaking into the cabinet or backing up the kitchen sink is urgent, and our line is answered 24/7. Switch off the unit and its breaker, close the sink valves if you can reach them and call for a same-day visit when a slot is open.

A garbage disposal grinds food waste so it can flow down the drain. When it jams, the motor hums without turning. When it dies, nothing happens at all. When a seal fails, water drips from the housing or the connections underneath. Each has a different cause and a different fix.

Never put your hand in a disposal. Turn it off at the switch, and for any repair, unplug it or switch off the breaker. If the unit is old and failing, replacement may be the better value.

Emergency: a disposal that leaks or floods the sink

A leaking disposal drips onto the cabinet floor and, unnoticed, onto the subfloor. A backed-up one can send greasy water over the rim. Power off first, then water.

Kitchen drains in seaside kitchens

Marblehead is a seafood-minded town, and fish skins, shells and fatty scraps are hard on a disposal and a drain. Shells in particular do not grind well, and they settle in the line. The simplest habit is to put shells and bones in the trash.

Older kitchens may have a single drain that serves the disposal, the dishwasher and sometimes a second sink, so a clog affects them all. The plumber looks at the whole line, not just the unit.

Install or replace a disposal according to the manufacturer's guidelines, and know that the drain connections fall under the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 while any new circuit falls under the electrical code. Ask about both before a circuit is added.

Humming and jammed

A hum with no spin means something is lodged between the impeller and the shredder ring. Most units have a hex socket in the center of the underside, where a wrench can turn the plate back and forth to free the jam. Pressing the reset button on the underside then restores power.

If the jam keeps returning, the disposal may be undersized for the load or the motor may be wearing out.

Dead disposal

Check the reset button, the switch and the breaker. A tripped overload protector is the most common cause. If power reaches the unit and it still will not run, the motor has probably failed, and replacement is the sensible option.

Leaks from the disposal

From the top flange

A worn gasket or sink flange seal.

From the side

The dishwasher inlet or drain hose.

From the bottom

A cracked housing, which means replacement.

From the drain pipe

A loose slip nut or washer.

Odors and clogs behind the disposal

A disposal that smells or drains slowly is usually pointing at the drain beyond it, where grease and food scraps build up. The unit grinds the food, but the pipe has to carry it. If the kitchen line backs up, drain cleaning fixes the cause.

What not to put down a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts.
  • Fibrous or starchy foods such as celery, potato peels and pasta.
  • Bones, pits and non-food objects.
