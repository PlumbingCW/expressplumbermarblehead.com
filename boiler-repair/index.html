Emergency boiler repair in Marblehead, MA

No heat on a cold night is a household emergency, and our line is answered 24/7. Check that the thermostat is calling for heat, that the boiler has power and fuel and that the pressure gauge reads in its normal range, then call. Same-day visits are offered when a slot is available.

Boiler repair means finding why the system is not making or moving heat and replacing the failed part. Common faults are an ignition problem, a failed circulator pump, low or high pressure, a stuck zone valve, an air-locked radiator or a leaking fitting.

Emergency: no heat in cold weather

A house that loses heat in a cold snap risks frozen pipes within hours. The first goal is to keep the building above freezing while the boiler is fixed.

Heating older Marblehead homes

Marblehead has a large stock of homes built long before modern heating, with systems that have been added to and adapted over decades. Radiators that heat unevenly, zones that were added later and boilers in cramped basements are all familiar.

Winter on the coast brings wind and damp, and a house that loses heat cools fast. The plumber also checks the pipes in exterior walls, because a heat failure is how many freeze breaks start.

Boiler and gas work in Massachusetts is held to state standards, and the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses the trades involved. Ask who holds the licence for the work on your boiler, and follow the guidelines in the manufacturer's manual for pressure and service intervals.

No heat: a short checklist

Start with the thermostat, the switch on the boiler and the fuel supply. An oil boiler needs oil in the tank and a reset button that has not tripped. A gas boiler needs the gas valve open and the pilot or igniter working.

If the boiler runs but the house stays cold, the problem is often a circulator pump that failed, a zone valve that did not open or air trapped in the loop. Those are plumber jobs, and we diagnose them on the first visit.

Pressure faults and leaks

A hot water boiler holds a set pressure, usually in the low teens when cold. Pressure that keeps falling means a leak or a failed expansion tank. Pressure that climbs and discharges the relief valve points to the same tank or to an overfilling valve.

A boiler that needs water added again and again is leaking somewhere, even if you cannot see it. We look at the fittings, the relief valve and the pipes along the way, and we tell you if the boiler itself is leaking.

Noises and what they mean

Banging or kettling

Scale or low water flow overheats the water in the heat exchanger.

Gurgling in radiators

Air in the loop needs bleeding or a purge.

Clicking at the valve

A zone valve motor may be failing.

Loud pump hum

A worn circulator can seize.

Never ignore a smell of gas or a sooty flue. Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. A boiler that vents badly can release carbon monoxide, and a working detector near the boiler room is essential.

Steam, hot water and older systems

Steam boilers have a sight glass, a low-water cutoff and radiator vents that wear out. Hot water systems use circulators, zone valves and expansion tanks. Older systems may combine both ideas with decades of patches.

Marblehead has many older houses, and old boilers are common. We explain what we find, and when a repair is likely to be followed by another one, we say so.

Repair or replace

A boiler that is under about 15 years old and has a single failed part is usually worth repairing. An older unit with a cracked heat exchanger or recurring faults is a replacement case. The plumber lays out both so you decide with the numbers in front of you.
