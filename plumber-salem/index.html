Salem's old houses and the plumbing inside them

Early houses, early pipe

Salem has houses that were built long before modern plumbing codes, and many have had their plumbing added in layers. Galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and mixed repairs are common. The plumber photographs what they find so you see the pipe, not just the invoice.

Mid-century and later

Later neighborhoods have copper supply lines and a mix of drain materials. Pinhole leaks, old solder joints and failing water heaters are typical. Leak detection and water heater replacement cover most calls.

Multifamily and mixed use

Salem's dense blocks include two- and three-family buildings and mixed-use storefronts. One shared line can serve several units, so a camera check helps assign the problem to the right fixture. See commercial plumbing for business work.

Where Salem's water comes from

Salem's water is supplied through the Salem and Beverly Water Supply Board, which draws from the Ipswich River and three reservoirs named Wenham Lake, Putnamville and Longham. The board treats the water at a plant on the shore of Wenham Lake in Beverly, and adjusts pH and adds phosphate for corrosion control.

According to the board's report, Salem's mains interconnect with Marblehead and Peabody, which is one reason a single crew can serve the three. For your own pipes, the useful point is that corrosion control chemistry protects metal pipe over time, but it cannot undo decades of buildup inside old galvanized lines.

Lead and service lines in an old city

The supply board's 2025 report states that Salem inventoried customer service pipe materials and records no identified lead exceedances, with a 90th-percentile lead result of 1.5 parts per billion in 2023 sampling. That is well under the 15 ppb action level.

A low citywide result does not tell you what is in your house. If the home is old, ask the Salem DPW or the city's water and sewer page about your service pipe, and see water service line repair if the line from the street needs work.

While the plumber travels to Salem

Salem houses often have basements with the main shutoff in a corner or behind stored items. Clear a path to it now, and these steps follow.

  • Water: Find and close the main shutoff.
  • Power: Switch off breakers to wet areas.
  • Drains: Stop all water use if sewage is backing up.
  • Access: Clear a path to the meter, heater or cleanout.

Drains and sewers in older Salem blocks

Dense blocks of old houses share trees, narrow lots and long-run drains. Roots find old clay and cast iron joints, and grease collects in older kitchen lines. Drain cleaning handles the clog, a camera inspection shows why it came back, and sewer line repair covers a defect.

Basement floor drains in older buildings can back up in heavy rain. A plumber looks at both the drain line and the sump setup before blaming one or the other.

Route 114 and getting to you

Route 114 connects Marblehead to Salem and carries the dispatch run. Traffic varies, and Salem fills up in October, so the dispatcher gives a realistic window when you call. The drive is about 5 miles and about 15 minutes as an estimate in light traffic.
