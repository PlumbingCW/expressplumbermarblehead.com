Repiping in Marblehead, MA

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes in a house, either throughout or in a section. It is usually the answer when a house has galvanized steel, polybutylene or heavily corroded copper that leaks again and again. The fix is bigger than a spot repair, and it should be explained clearly before it begins.

The signs are consistent. Rust-colored water, low pressure that has been creeping down, pinhole leaks at different places and visible green or white buildup on pipes all point to supply lines near the end of their useful life.

Repiping in Marblehead's older housing

The town's own history notes that building slowed to a stop around 1970, so much of its housing is older than modern supply pipe standards. A house with the original galvanized pipe is a realistic repipe candidate, and so is one with a patchwork of materials from many repairs.

Plaster walls, narrow stair halls and finished basements make access careful work in old houses. We plan routes through closets and unfinished areas first to keep the disturbance down.

Why old pipes fail

Galvanized steel is steel pipe with a zinc coating that wears off. The pipe then rusts from the inside, and the buildup narrows it until pressure collapses. Polybutylene, a gray plastic used for a period in the late twentieth century, becomes brittle and can burst at fittings.

Copper lasts a long time, but acidic water, poor solder and high velocity can cause pinholes. A single pinhole is a repair. A pattern of pinholes along the run is a reason to replace the run.

Copper or PEX

Copper is a durable, familiar material with a long track record. PEX is a flexible plastic that can run through walls with fewer joints and can tolerate some freezing without splitting. Both are accepted in Massachusetts plumbing code when installed correctly.

The best choice depends on the house, the budget and the walls. We explain the trade-offs, including the access and the repair work to drywall.

How a repipe is done

The plumber plans the new routes, shuts off the water, opens small access holes in walls and ceilings, runs the new pipe and reconnects every fixture. Water is off during the day, and we explain how long.

A permit and inspection are normal for a repipe. Marblehead's Building Department issues plumbing permits to licensed plumbers, and starting without a required permit triggers a fee of three times the normal amount.

Partial repiping

Not every house needs a full repipe. A failing run to one bathroom or a single section of the basement can be replaced alone, and often that is the right first step. We tell you what we find and give you the choice.
