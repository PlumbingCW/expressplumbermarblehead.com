Sewer camera inspection in Marblehead, MA

A sewer camera inspection feeds a small waterproof camera on a cable through the sewer line, so the plumber can see the pipe from the inside. It finds roots, cracks, offsets, bellies, scale and debris, and records where each one sits along the line.

Homeowners book inspections when drains clog more than once, when a line has been cleaned and they want to know why it clogged, or when they are buying a house with an old sewer lateral. A recording and a written note give you something concrete to act on.

Why inspect in a town like Marblehead

Marblehead is one of the older settled towns on the North Shore, and a house of any age can sit on a lateral line that has never been looked at. A buyer in Old Town, on the Neck or in Clifton has good reason to want a recording before closing.

Rock and tight lots make digging costly, so knowing exactly where a defect sits before a quote is worth more here than in a flat, open suburb.

A camera inspection is recorded so the finding can be reviewed later, and repairs that follow are held to the standards of the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, along with the town's rules for sewer connections. Ask for the video and a written summary.

What the camera reveals

Roots look like fine white threads or heavy mats at a joint. A crack shows as a dark line, sometimes with soil behind it. An offset joint is a visible step, and a belly shows as standing water at a low point. Scale and grease coat the walls with a rough layer.

A locator at the camera head lets the plumber mark the spot above ground, so a repair can be planned for the right place.

Inspecting before you buy

A house inspection does not usually include the sewer lateral. An old lateral may be clay, cast iron or an early plastic that has failed. Finding that out before closing is much cheaper than finding it after.

If the camera shows a major defect, you can ask the seller for a credit or a repair. Ask your attorney how to handle it in the contract.

When to book an inspection

Repeat clogs

More than one blockage in a year points to a defect.

After a big clean

Confirm the result and check what caused it.

Before excavation or a driveway

Know where the line runs and what shape it is in.

Older home, never inspected

A baseline recording is useful for years.

What you receive

You get the video, a plain-language summary and the distance to each defect. If a repair is needed, the next step and the choices are explained. If the line is sound, you get that in writing too.

Limits of a camera

A camera cannot see through a full blockage, so the line may need clearing first. Very small or sharp-turn pipes may limit access. In those cases we say what could not be seen.
