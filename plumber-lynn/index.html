Plumbing through the seasons in Lynn

Winter: freezes and no heat

Cold snaps send the calls in. Frozen exterior pipes, failed boilers and burst lines make up most of the winter volume. See burst pipe repair and boiler repair.

Spring: storms and basements

Spring rain fills pits and floor drains. A pump that fails in a storm is an emergency, and low basements take in water first. See sump pump repair.

Summer and fall: drains and heaters

Heavy use means more grease, more laundry and more tank failures. Backups and no-hot-water calls rise. See drain cleaning and water heater replacement.

Who supplies Lynn's water

Lynn's water is run by the Lynn Water and Sewer Commission, which also takes away and processes wastewater. According to a state source water assessment report, the commission maintains six public water supply sources: Breeds Pond, Hawkes Pond, Birch Pond, Walden Pond, and intakes on the Ipswich River and the Saugus River.

The report lists the Raymond Reardon Water Treatment Plant on Parkland Avenue. Lynn runs its own sources, so a plumber working across the coast keeps each town's water system straight when a customer asks about taste or sediment.

Old multifamily housing and shared lines

Lynn has a large stock of older two- and three-family houses and apartment buildings. Shared drain stacks and a single water heater feeding several units are common, and the cause of a problem in one unit often sits in another. A camera and a careful trace save repeated visits.

For multi-unit buildings we ask who the water heater and the main line serve before sending a crew. See sewer camera inspection and commercial plumbing for landlord work.

Winter on the coast

Wind off the water pushes cold into poorly sealed exterior walls, and pipes in garages, porches and crawlspaces freeze first. Open a faucet slightly on the coldest nights if a line has frozen before, and keep cabinet doors open on exterior walls. A pipe that has frozen once should be checked for cracks after it thaws.

Sewer backups and storm drains

The Lynn Water and Sewer Commission handles the public sewer, so a backup that starts in the street main is the commission's to clear. A backup that starts inside your lateral is yours. A cleanout outside with standing water and a clear street main points to your side, and the plumber clears it and records what they find.

After heavy rain, older basements with floor drains are the first to back up. Keep stored items off the floor and test your sump pump before the season. See drain cleaning for the usual first steps.
