Water service line repair in Marblehead, MA

A service line that has burst or is losing pressure fast is an emergency, and our line is answered 24/7. Close the main shutoff, keep clear of any wet or sinking ground and call for a same-day visit when a slot is open.

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the town main in the street to your house. Where it ends and who owns what matters, so we start by confirming where the meter sits and which side the problem is on. Pressure that fades over weeks, a soft wet spot in the lawn or a bill that keeps climbing are the usual signs of a failing line.

Repair can mean a spot fix at a joint, a short replacement of the damaged run, or a full replacement from the street to the house. The camera, the pressure test and the dig location together decide which.

Emergency: a failing service line

A broken service line can flood a basement or wash out the ground around it. Closing the main valve limits the water, and the dispatcher can talk you through finding it.

Our emergency plumbers answer 24/7 and offer same-day service when available, including after hours. The steps below hold things until they arrive.

  • Main valve offTurn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Check the meterIf the dial still moves, the leak is on the supply side.
  • Protect the basementLift stored items and keep power off near any water.
  • Call the lineSay where the water is showing and what pressure you have.

Water supply in Marblehead

Marblehead buys all its water from the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority, which means the town's main is fed through a regional system. The water arrives at consistent quality, but your service line is the last stretch it passes through, and an old galvanized line can affect taste, color and flow even when the supply is good.

For the town's own water quality information, the annual report from the Water and Sewer Department is the source. A plumber can tell you what the pipe looks like, and the department can tell you what the water contains.

Who is responsible for which section

In most towns the water department owns the main and the connection up to the meter or shutoff, and the homeowner owns the line from there into the house. Marblehead's Water and Sewer Department can tell you where that boundary sits for your address.

Call the department before you dig or replace anything. If the leak sits on the town side, the department handles it. If it sits on the house side, a plumber does the work and pulls any permit that applies.

Lead and galvanized services

The town's published water information says it has no known lead service lines, but that a few homes have galvanized iron services which may include a short lead gooseneck connection. If your house is old, ask the department what material your service is and when it was installed.

A galvanized service corrodes over time, restricting flow and eventually leaking. Replacement with copper or a modern plastic service also restores pressure, which is often the first thing a homeowner notices.

Digging in Marblehead's ground

Marblehead sits on rock, with ledge close to the surface in many places, and that shapes how a service line job is planned. We locate the line, check for ledge and utilities, and tell you if trenchless methods or a different route make sense.

Utility marking is a legal step before any excavation, and we build it into the schedule. Digging in a road or sidewalk may also need a town permit.

Pressure, valves and the meter box

A failing shutoff valve at the meter is often found alongside a line problem. We check that the main shutoff turns, that the pressure reducing valve, if there is one, is holding the right pressure and that the meter box is not hiding a leak.
